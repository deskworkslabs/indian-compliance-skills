# POSH Compliance (India)

Helps an organisation operating in India meet its obligations under the
**Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and
Redressal) Act, 2013** ("POSH Act") and the associated 2013 Rules. This
skill is a compliance-process guide, not legal advice — a qualified
advocate or the organisation's own legal counsel should review anything
that will be filed, relied on in a dispute, or shown to a court or
District Officer.

## When to use this skill

- Setting up an **Internal Committee (IC)** for the first time, or
  re-constituting one after a 3-year term expires
- Drafting or reviewing a **POSH policy** for an employee handbook
- Structuring the intake, inquiry, and resolution of a **complaint**
- Preparing the **annual report** an employer must file
- Checking whether an organisation is covered, and what its specific
  obligations are, based on headcount and structure

## Core obligations the skill checks for

1. **Applicability** — the Act covers every employer with **10 or more
   employees** at a workplace, but a few duties (the policy, the
   display of IC details, the right to a safe workplace) apply even
   below that threshold, and in that case complaints route to the
   district-level **Local Committee (LC)** instead of an IC.
2. **Internal Committee composition** (Section 4) — mandatory seats:
   - A **Presiding Officer**: a senior woman employee. If no senior
     woman employee is available at that location, the Act permits
     nominating one from another office of the same organisation.
   - At least **two employee members** "committed to the cause of
     women" or with experience in social work / legal knowledge.
   - **One external member** from an NGO, association, or a person
     familiar with sexual harassment issues — not an employee of the
     organisation. This is the seat most often filled incorrectly
     (an internal HR person mislabelled as "external") or left vacant.
   - **At least half the total members must be women.**
3. **Term and documentation** — IC members serve a maximum of **3
   years**; keep dated appointment letters, not just an org chart.
4. **Complaint timeline** (Sections 9–12) — a complaint must be filed
   within **3 months** of the incident (extendable by the IC up to 3
   more months for sufficient reason); the IC must complete its
   inquiry within **90 days** of the complaint; the employer must act
   on the IC's recommendation within **60 days** of receiving it.
   Conciliation (Section 10) is available only if the complainant
   requests it, and only for non-monetary settlement — the IC cannot
   push conciliation on an unwilling complainant.
5. **Confidentiality** (Section 16) — the identity of the complainant,
   respondent, witnesses, and the inquiry contents may not be
   published or disclosed outside the proceedings; breach is itself
   punishable under the Rules.
6. **Annual report** (Section 21, Rule 14) — every IC must submit an
   annual report to the employer, who forwards a summary to the
   District Officer; must include at minimum: number of complaints
   received, number disposed of, number pending beyond 90 days, and
   number of workshops/awareness programmes conducted.
7. **Annual training** — the employer must organise awareness
   programmes and sensitise employees and IC members at regular
   intervals (Section 19).

## Workflow

### 1. Applicability check
Ask for headcount, number of locations, and whether any location has
fewer than 10 employees. Flag which locations need a full IC vs. which
route to the state's Local Committee, and that the written policy,
workplace display requirement, and safe-workplace duty apply
regardless of size.

### 2. IC composition review
Given a proposed member list, check each of the four seat rules above
by name and role, flag any seat that is vacant, filled by someone
ineligible (e.g. an "external member" who is actually a retained
consultant or ex-employee — this does not satisfy the independence
intent of Section 4(1)(c)), or past the 3-year term.

### 3. Policy drafting
Produce a POSH policy section covering: definition of sexual
harassment per Section 2(n) and 3, scope (employees, contractors,
visitors, remote/hybrid work, off-site events, work-related travel),
how and where to file a complaint, IC contact details, the 3-month
filing window, interim relief available under Section 12 (transfer,
leave, restraining contact — up to 3 months, pending inquiry), the
inquiry process outline, confidentiality obligation, and the
penalties for false or malicious complaints (Section 14) — stated
carefully so this clause is not used to discourage genuine complaints.

### 4. Complaint handling structure
When asked to help structure (not adjudicate) a specific complaint:
produce a neutral intake template (date, nature of complaint, parties,
witnesses, remedy sought), the day-count timeline the IC must track
against (filing date, extension if granted, 90-day inquiry deadline,
60-day employer-action deadline), and a reminder that the IC — not
this skill, not HR, not legal counsel alone — conducts the inquiry
and reaches findings. Never draft or suggest the substantive outcome
of a specific inquiry; that is the IC's statutory role.

### 5. Annual report
Produce the annual report in the Rule 14 format given the year's
complaint counts, disposals, pending-past-90-days count, and
awareness programmes held.

## What this skill does not do

- It does not replace the IC's inquiry — it structures process and
  paperwork around a human committee that must exercise its own
  judgement.
- It does not give a legal opinion on whether specific conduct
  constitutes sexual harassment in a live case; flag that question to
  the organisation's advocate.
- It does not file anything with a District Officer or a court —
  output is a draft for the organisation's own review and filing.
